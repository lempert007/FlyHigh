How FlyHigh Plans Your Drone's Altitude

A plain-English walkthrough of the 9-step altitude algorithm in core/altitude.py

What the algorithm produces

Drone flight path
Takeoff / Return home
Min AGL floor
Max AGL ceiling
Ramp start point
Terrain pin (ramp safety)
Zone crossing
Home / Land

The 9 steps — grouped by what they do

🛫 Preparation (Steps 1–2) 📐 Planning (Step 3–4) 🎯 Selection (Step 5–6) 🔧 Build output (Steps 7–8) ✅ Verification (Step 9)
1
Resolve takeoff altitude
Decide how high the drone is at the moment it takes off.

If the user pinned a fixed altitude, use that. Otherwise, read the terrain elevation at the home point and add the minimum AGL clearance on top — the lowest safe starting height.
Preparation
2a
Insert zone-crossing points
Each POI area can have its own altitude rules (e.g. "fly lower here"). Whenever the route crosses into or out of a POI zone, a hidden waypoint is inserted at that exact boundary.

This ensures no single flight leg ever spans two different rule-sets at once.
Preparation
2b
Split impossible legs
Sometimes terrain varies so wildly on one leg that no single flat altitude can satisfy both "stay above the peak" and "stay below the ceiling".

The leg is split at its midpoint and each half is checked again — up to 3 times (making at most 8 sub-legs). After that a violation is logged.
Preparation
3
Compute floor & ceiling per leg
For each leg the terrain is sampled at 20 m intervals.

Floor = highest terrain point + min AGL — the drone must be at least this high.
Ceiling = lowest terrain point + max AGL — the drone must not exceed this.

Step 2b guarantees floor ≤ ceiling for every leg.
Planning
4
Propagate climb-rate limits
The drone can't teleport — it climbs and descends at a limited slope. Step 4 runs 4 fast passes over the leg list to tighten each leg's allowed altitude range so that every transition is reachable:

• backward & forward floor passes — "start climbing early enough"
• backward & forward ceiling passes — "start descending early enough"
Planning
5
Select cruise altitude
A single forward sweep assigns one altitude to each leg. The rule is simple:

Keep the current altitude unless forced to change.

The drone only climbs when the floor rises above it, and only descends when the ceiling drops below it. Fewer changes = smoother, more efficient flight.
Selection
6
Clamp takeoff altitude
Now that the first leg's cruise altitude is known, the takeoff altitude from Step 1 is bumped up if it would be too low to reach the first leg's floor in time.

(Only applies when the user hasn't pinned a fixed takeoff altitude.)
Selection
7
Insert ramp waypoints
Between any two legs at different altitudes, a ramp start point is inserted within the preceding leg. The drone flies flat, then begins climbing or descending at the exact moment needed to arrive at the new altitude right on time.

Because Step 4 already guaranteed the slope fits, no backwards walking is required.
Build
8
Terrain pins on ramps
During a ramp the drone's altitude is interpolated linearly. A hill hidden mid-ramp could be clipped even if the endpoints are fine.

Step 8 samples every ramp segment and, if the worst clearance violation is found, inserts a terrain pin waypoint there. This repeats until the ramp is clean.
Build
9
Final safety check
Every segment in the completed route is sampled at 20 m intervals one last time.

Any point where the drone would be below min AGL is a hard violation.
Any point where the drone would be above max AGL is a soft warning.

Violations are reported in the results panel but never block the download.
Verification

🔒 Key guarantees after all 9 steps

  • Every flat leg is within the min–max AGL band.
  • Every altitude transition respects the drone's max climb/descent rate.
  • Every ramp segment has at least min AGL clearance (via terrain pins).
  • Where constraints are physically impossible, a violation is logged — the route is still delivered.
